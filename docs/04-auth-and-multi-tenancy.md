## Initial milestone authorization scope

Owners can select admins from existing approved `community_accounts` in their tenant under the October 30 refinement, linked to verified global users. The assignment flag (`can_create_admin` in the implemented milestone; target `can_grant_staff` under D-106) also permits active same-tenant admins to perform this controlled assignment. Enforce the target's same-tenant community association and verified identity server-side; create an admin assignment with all permissions false without changing community standing or benefits. Repeated creation must not overwrite existing assignments or their permissions. Staff identity retains its direct global-account FK; all effective assignments require an approved community account throughout their lifetime. Ownership now requires an approved community account; the earlier opt-out exception is superseded.

Support this workflow with a narrow administrative candidate list available only to the owner or staff with `can_grant_staff`: same-tenant account user ID, activity, standing, existing staff-assignment status, and optional profile display name. Omit bio, email, audit actors, and unrelated profile data; a missing profile does not prevent selection. Similarly, approval and reinstatement workflows may expose same-tenant pending/removed account IDs, activity/standing, and optional display names to effective active staff with `can_approve_users`; restrict these projections to their management purpose. These are explicit administrative projections, not general directory/profile access. Profile reads/edits otherwise remain self-only for this milestone; directory access waits for community audience/privacy policies, not paid entitlements.

The owner-only permission command changes `can_approve_users`, `can_create_admin`, and, with the locally and hosted-applied 2026-10-24 migration, `can_modify_content` on active admins. Omitted/null parameters leave grants unchanged. D-106's staff-grant rename and removal/revocation additions remain planned; preserve audit records and always-true owner flags. Full staff configuration UI and configurable defaults remain later work. Tenant details remain owner-only, and settings remain owner-only until the planned `can_manage_settings` migration. Website-copy editing now uses the separate content grant described below. `can_grant_staff` will also authorize manual paused-admin resumption, without permission-editing authority.

Implemented community-account commands cover joining and approval only; D-097–D-103 add planned deactivation/reactivation and their coordinated consequences. Suspension/removal/reinstatement commands are deferred; rejoining cannot reset standing. Admin invitations (including their persistence, delivery, redemption, and UI) are a follow-up feature. References to invitation/transfer tests elsewhere in this document describe future acceptance requirements, not additional first-milestone scope. Use `tenant_admin_actions` as the canonical tenant-scoped administrative audit table.

## Identity, standing, benefits, and participation

[accepted community-account column contract](16-intended-access-matrix.md#community-account-columns--accepted-2026-10-30) distinguishes peer-readable standing/restriction/audit from self preferences and permission-manager fields. Existing tenant/directory/history/archive rules determine account rows. Private helpers may evaluate target preferences for RLS/operations without exposing raw settings or qualifying affiliations. Derive the caller from authentication and enforce global-profile blocks; knowing an ID or audit actor grants no additional row access. Restriction status does not open disciplinary evidence/history.

[accepted global-account column contract](16-intended-access-matrix.md#global-account-columns--accepted-2026-10-30) keeps global account settings self-readable across all eight current columns. Self may update only the three global preferences; trusted provisioning owns insertion and identity/audit attribution. Other profile/identity audiences do not gain the raw settings row. Permission helpers may evaluate the relevant preference for an operation, retaining independent discovery/matching/DM/share rules and existing caller checks. Community staff status supplies no general global-settings access.

[accepted community-profile column contract](16-intended-access-matrix.md#community-profile-columns--accepted-2026-10-30) permits all eight current columns for existing full-community-profile audiences, including audit actors. Caller identity, tenant eligibility, profile lifecycle, historical-link and archive rules still control rows. Clients supply/change only their own display_name/bio; trusted creation derives tenant/account identity and stamps audit fields. Public identity projections do not gain full-profile access. Community blocks retain their already-settled profile visibility behavior.

[accepted global-profile column contract](16-intended-access-matrix.md#global-profile-columns--accepted-2026-10-30) separates column grants from row eligibility: full global-profile readers receive all seven current columns, including audit UUIDs/timestamps, only when the existing audience/block checks authorize that row. Clients insert/update only their own display_name/bio; identity/audit are trusted. Metadata visibility grants no referenced-actor profile or administrative-history access. Community-profile audit visibility is now separately accepted in its column contract above.

Supabase Auth identifies users. Community activity/standing, explicit staff grants, and resource participation are distinct. All effective staff, including owner, require an approved same-tenant community account. An active account alone grants neither staff permissions nor approval to participate. Paid membership is a deferred concept and is not part of current authorization.

Proposed tenant: an independently operated community, initially Tegucigalpa. Locations and member tables are not tenants. Community business authorization is tenant-specific, even for a user associated with multiple communities; global-profile self-management and main-account blocks are account-scoped. Future independent matchmaking/hosting require their own resource audiences and commands, not an assumed community association. The intended UX includes self-service tenant creation as described below. A tenant switcher is not currently required for the first release.

The initial sign-in method is email/password through Supabase Auth. OAuth/OIDC sign-in is planned for a later release and is outside this milestone. Supabase Auth remains authoritative for credentials and login identities; do not duplicate passwords or verification tokens in application tables. The email/password implementation must include password recovery and reliable PWA callback behavior. See [Supabase Auth](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/auth).

Initial hosted testing uses project `fsnipgsrlclbnfepcbvz` with a localhost frontend (`http://localhost:5173`). Authentication emails are English initially; multilingual templates are deferred. Resend is selected for initial SMTP delivery, and `thecrowdedtable.app` was purchased through Cloudflare Registrar. See the [Auth operations record](11-auth-operations.md) for the recommended sender subdomain, dashboard settings, and review triggers. See the [hosted setup status](../supabase/README.md#selected-hosted-testing-environment) before treating this as an operational environment.

After a new signup is successfully authenticated, send the user to **My Home**, the personal application landing page (2026-10-03 clarification), rather than a community's home page. For this initial flow, do not resume a pre-signup destination, send the user directly to community creation, or automatically create a tenant/join a community/accept a staff invitation. Account creation and community onboarding remain separate explicit actions. Password recovery retains its dedicated recovery flow rather than being treated as a new-signup redirect.

Email verification is required to complete account creation. Explicitly enable email confirmation in every environment, including local development. Signup initially shows a check-your-email screen with a resend option; only after verification and successful authentication does the user proceed to the home page. Auth and a corresponding `user_accounts` record may be provisioned before verification, but these are pending signup records, not authorization to use authenticated application features. Do not add a duplicate application-level verification flag; Supabase Auth is authoritative.

Until verified, users may browse permitted public content and complete verification/recovery flows, but may not create tenants, join communities, accept admin invitations, or perform authenticated management/participation actions. Enforce this boundary through Auth configuration and backend authorization rather than UI controls alone. Verification is a platform-wide requirement and does not replace community approval or grant membership benefits. Callback route names, environment-specific redirect allowlists, production sender/SMTP configuration, and recovery details remain implementation/setup decisions; the post-signup destination is settled as home.

## Account and community creation

October 30 access clarification: ordinary inspection of another community profile requires `is_active = true` and approved/suspended standing in that community. Pending, inactive/removed and non-member viewers use public content rules and cannot fetch profiles. The default-false `allow_public_directory_visibility` grants only public directory rows for active approved/suspended targets, not profile access; minimal identity presentation is display name plus active staff-role badge, with exact visuals/fields still to design. Existing self onboarding and authorized management are separate. Deactivating a community account removes its pending join request, with no stale approval; storage and concurrency design remain future work.

1. A first-time visitor can browse permitted public content without an account.
2. **Create Account** starts email/password signup, provisioning the Auth identity and its global `user_accounts` record. Require email verification before signup is complete and authenticated app access is available; then send the authenticated user to My Home. No community account or tenant association is required.
3. **Start New Community** creates the tenant and automatically assigns the signed-in creator as its single active owner through `tenant_staff_assignments`. These writes must succeed or fail together in a trusted transaction.
4. October 30 supersedes the former optional participation question: ownership must create/approve the corresponding community account and create/update the active owner assignment in the same transaction as tenant creation. The UI may not offer a path leaving the owner without an approved community account. Reconcile curated-profile onboarding fields separately with D-112; owner authority adds no ability to edit the global profile incidentally.
5. Joining an existing community uses the same editable pre-fill and submits the community profile with the join request. The verified caller's new account/profile commit together, with pending/approved standing determined by community policy. Missing global fields can be entered directly; joining does not implicitly save or alter a global profile. Existing-account return/reinstatement retains its separate standing-preserving policy and cannot silently overwrite an existing profile.

There is one global login. Staff assignments and community participation are separate tenant-specific relationships. Joining does not automatically grant paid membership benefits. The creator's optional community account is automatically approved during tenant creation regardless of `tenant_settings.auto_approve_users`. Create the default settings row with the tenant and owner assignment in the same transaction.

The implementation now has global and community profile tables, with real global-profile editing; community joining still permits later profile setup. D-111 adds `user_account_profiles` keyed by the global account, while retaining curated `community_account_profiles` keyed by tenant/user. Both initially have `display_name`, optional `bio`, and standard audit fields. D-112 changes new UI joins to submit account/profile together; legacy profile-less accounts require a setup path. Profile presence still grants no participation or staff authority, and account creation itself does not require a completed profile. Global-profile edits depend on verified account ownership rather than a tenant's lifecycle; community-profile writes retain community lifecycle/standing gates.

Intra-community visibility of the curated community profile is a mandatory requirement of community participation (AM-01, accepted 2026-10-27). No separate sharing opt-in or opt-out applies to that community presentation. The self-controlled `community_accounts.show_global_profile` flag defaults false (D-113). It controls global-profile visibility through that community's authorized audience, independently of its curated community profile. Global `user_accounts.user_is_discoverable` defaults true and controls whether other users can find the account in standard global account searches. Separate global `user_accounts.enable_matchmaking` defaults true and controls participation in app-wide matchmaking and competitions (D-114, clarified by AM-02). All are non-null booleans; the two global flags belong on the account, not the profile or community-account row.

AM-02 (accepted 2026-10-27): any authenticated user may search global profiles. `user_accounts.user_is_discoverable = false` excludes the target from global search without preventing independently authorized visibility through a community or engagement in app-wide matchmaking/competitions. The existing `user_accounts.enable_matchmaking` flag (default true) governs both app-wide matchmaking and competitions; no separate competition flag is needed. Community-mediated global visibility evaluates all qualifying shared communities: one target `community_accounts.show_global_profile = true` row suffices, even when the current community's row is false. A known URL grants nothing. Retain block/lifecycle checks and do not disclose unrelated affiliations. Do not combine these three controls into one visibility predicate.

Intra-community matchmaking and competitions operate on community accounts and their curated profiles. Participation in either does not itself authorize inspection of the corresponding global account/profile, and the global matchmaking flag is not a community-activity gate. An independent global-profile access path must qualify separately. Activity visibility is not global-search inclusion or access to raw account settings, audit fields, Auth data or unrelated affiliations. Each feature must enforce its own live preference plus applicable audience, account, and block rules; none grants DM/invitation permission. October 30 settles URL non-authority and any-qualifying-shared-community composition. Exact activity viewers/fields remain Q-016/AM-02. AM-08 settles pending-work opt-out versus retained commitments, refresh/navigation display updates and recognition within authorized history. Current self-only account/profile reads are deployed milestone behavior, not the intended general audience. Resolve the remaining audience rules and implement their authorization contract independently of frontend readiness; do not preserve temporary restrictions by adding unnecessary privileged workarounds.

Blocks, discipline, and private label targets resolve the internal `user_accounts.user_id` even when the global profile is hidden. Names, bios, rejoining, label edits, and preference changes cannot bypass account-level blocks; staff disciplinary authority remains local. D-115 selects private labels and account-level blocking instead of a public recognition ID. Only a label's owner can read/manage it; neither the labelled user nor community staff receive access. Labels require no block and grant no additional access to target profiles, affiliations, or reports. Authorize the encountered context before displaying private recognition. D-116 settles search/matching, direct-invitation, and joining restrictions below; residual lifecycle questions remain Q-010. Any future claim of limited linkability must account for identifiers exposed by APIs, author metadata, and subscriptions, not just rendered UI.

When either account blocks the other, exclude each from the other's global account searches and casual global matchmaking, and deny direct invitations between them in both directions. These restrictions are pair-specific and independent of `user_is_discoverable`, `enable_matchmaking`, `show_global_profile`, or invitation opt-in. They do not make either account unavailable to unrelated users or revoke community standing. Each person manages only their own directed block; removing one does not clear a reverse block.

Personal blocks do not prevent community joining or otherwise accessible participation outside D-118's private host/participant exclusion. Private events and tables deny admission if the actual host/organizer and participant have a block in either direction, through every route including links, third-party invitations, and parent-event registration. Use resource hosting, not community ownership, generic staff authority, or audit authorship. Other ordinary attendees' blocks do not create a joining veto. D-118 extends the earlier private-table rule to private events and automatically removes existing counterpart participation/requests and rejects related invitations in not-yet-started resources when confirming a block under D-119; AM-08 separately settles opt-out commitments and exempts competitive participation from automatic block consequences; casual-resource mapping remains feature design.

D-119 evaluates the actual attendee, never the booking account as a proxy for a guest. Guests can participate independently despite a block involving their sponsor, including guest-only bookings in the other account's private gatherings. Preserve guest rows, group/booker links, and ordinary authorized guest-booking management when removing a sponsor's own participation. A block against the sponsor alone cannot reject or cancel guest participation. In-progress events/tables, existing participation/invitations, and their normal authorized access are unaffected by the block action; new-interaction gates remain separate from retained participation rights.

The block preview must tell the caller which not-yet-started private events/tables hosted by the target they will automatically leave and which not-yet-started private events/tables they host will automatically lose the target's participation. Show related mandatory invitation rejections and dependent seats. These exact automatic transitions are authorized by the block policy and cannot be deselected; they grant no general resource-management or staff power. Show only safe authorized details. Manual per-item withdrawal/cancellation/denial/invitation controls in this interface are limited to not-yet-started non-private resources and remain subject to the caller's normal permissions. Unrelated third-party private gatherings are not swept into automatic removal merely because both accounts attend, and receive no manual cancellation controls in this block interface.

Final confirmation applies the block, mandatory private effects, and optional selected non-private actions consistently; dismissing the prompt changes nothing. No manual selection is required, but all mandatory private effects still apply. Remove affected pending and confirmed participation/requests only for not-yet-started resources and reject pending invitations, preserving rows/history; withdrawing an event registration clears that attendee's linked upcoming table seats without touching guests or in-progress participation/required access. Recheck live lifecycle and start times at confirmation and refresh the preview if a gathering has started. Those removals end participation-based live resource/chat/address access while independent staff/organizer and retained historical audiences keep their own rules. Unselected non-private, independent guest, in-progress, and unaffected third-party private records remain under normal authorization. Unblocking restores none of the cancelled/rejected items; new participation/invitations require current eligibility.

Tenant slugs are lowercase, unique, exclude reserved application names, and are immutable after creation in the initial version. Validate creation at the backend/database; ownership does not permit slug changes. The migrations enforce 3–63 ASCII letters/digits with single separating hyphens and enumerate reserved route names; keep the reserved list synchronized with routing changes.

## Event and table access — decisions of 2026-10-12

[suspension and clearance decisions](16-intended-access-matrix.md#suspension-and-clearance--accepted-2026-10-30) supersede older blanket approved-only activity eligibility where they conflict. Suspension preserves private creation/reservation and private commitments under normal resource rules, blocks new community-public creation/reservations, and leaves existing public commitments for individual cancellation choices by the disciplining staff user. Do not apply this activity exception automatically to chat sending or staff authority. Suspension of active/paused staff requires both discipline permissions and revokes staff atomically; clearance changes suspended to approved, but inactive stays inactive pending separately authorized reactivation.

AM-08 competitive-play distinction (2026-10-28): global and community competitions use communication-only personal-block effects. Do not filter competitive opponents, revoke entries/pairings, auto-withdraw/forfeit or change results because of a block. Competitive actions beyond communication must be performed manually under the domain rules. Casual global matching follows the applicable community block model; ordinary resource/guest/in-progress protections remain. Competition-linked records must be distinguished from regular casual tables before invoking shared block cascades. AM-08 clarification: full global profiles remain mutually blocked in competition contexts. The identity needed for authorized pairings/results remains visible, without granting current global-profile inspection or overriding separate community-profile rules.

AM-07 update (2026-10-28): non-members, including pending applicants, may read community-account-hosted activities when `allow_public_activity_visibility` is true. Official activities remain visible independently of that flag. `allow_public_chat_visibility` separately grants non-members read-only access to community-public chats, including official-activity chats. Both settings default false and use `can_manage_settings` for edits. These grants supersede blanket non-member activity visibility and prior exclusion of all non-participant chat readers. They confer no posting/booking/profile/address authority. Signed-out visitors are included and official activities have no private-visibility exception; future participation gates do not hide official activities. October 30 adds public directory visibility, makes inactive/removed viewers public, and settles cancelled-but-not-archived public activity visibility with the activity flag and official exception; remaining chat lifecycle details stay in AM-07.

The [event/table schema direction](03-database-schema.md#events-tables-locations-and-seats) supersedes the older activity-subtype and paid-member-only participation assumptions where they conflict. Non-member activity/chat reads now follow the AM-07 update above. Cancellation/archive refinements below were confirmed on 2026-10-13. Participating account holders must be approved and not banned in that community; current `suspended`/`removed` standing fails that condition. Unofficial private/invite-only resources, invitation records, private addresses and profile data do not become public through this rule. Official activities remain visible even if a future participation policy requires an invitation. Event approval and capacity remain required; payment and paid membership checks are deferred.

Approved account holders can create guest bookings when `tables.allow_guests` is true (default true); false blocks new guest bookings/rebookings while preserving existing ones and their ordinary eligibility. Guest names are optional. Sponsors may register guests without registering themselves for the event or table; the app does not require them to attend/play or implement community/table sponsor-attendance policies. Use one event registration per attendee and one table participation per person, tracking the booking account separately. Event-associated participation references the occupant's exact registration; standalone participation stores the attendee account or optional guest name directly. A sponsored guest is not the same concept as the older matrix's signed-in, nonpaying account, and no guest seat grants a login, community standing, or independent chat/profile access.

Only an authorized manager of the table may edit `allow_guests` or explicitly remove its guest bookings. The flag edit grants no removal authority and performs no cancellation. The UI offers optional selection of existing guests when disabling the flag, plus manual removal through standard table management at any time. Removal affects selected table participations only; it does not confer authority to cancel event registrations or bookings at other tables. Private-view permission and cancellation/archive moderation of a public user event do not grant table-editing or guest-removal powers. Official-resource management instead requires `can_manage_official_activities`.

The occupant's approved event registration must precede seating at an associated table; a nonattending sponsor needs no attending registration. Explicitly cancelling an attendee's registration clears that person's linked table participations; an explicit group cancellation covers every selected registration. For unofficial resources only event organizers may add tables initially; public moderation alone is insufficient. Official event/table creation and management require staff with `can_manage_official_activities`. Cancelling the event or table itself leaves registration/participation rows unchanged; current access must also account for the resource and parent lifecycle.

Groups must be accepted together in one atomic transaction. Authorize the operation against the complete persisted group, validate every booking/attending account and guest's applicable eligibility, and check full capacity before any member becomes approved/confirmed. A caller cannot bypass group acceptance by supplying a subset of registration/participation IDs. The booking-account relationship grants no staff powers and does not treat a nonattending sponsor as a seated active-table participant. Guest bookers do receive cancelled/archived history and read-only chat access under the accepted historical-access rule, but no participant-based address access. See the [accepted identity and group structure](03-database-schema.md#per-person-registration-and-participation--accepted-structure).

Seat confirmation uses `tables.manually_confirm_seats`, default false. Automatic tables confirm eligible, non-withdrawn whole groups in receipt order as capacity allows. Manual tables require an authorized manager's approval, rejection, or issue notification; the manager may select requests at their discretion but cannot bypass full capacity, event approval, or atomic grouping. Neither mode requires booker reconfirmation or uses an expiry/response window.

Rejected requests leave the active queue completely, with retained decision history. The booking account receives a prompt decision notice with server timestamp, rejecting user, and optional reason. Expose this actor identity through a narrow authorized decision projection, not unrestricted staff profile/email reads. Issue notices leave the request pending. Only table-management authority permits these actions; private-view and unofficial-resource cancellation/archive powers remain insufficient.

Cancelled community-public events/tables remain discoverable to anyone, including anonymous visitors, through a Cancelled filter and safe public details; private resources preserve their original organizer/invitee audience and authorized staff oversight. Historical registration/participation rows additionally authorize their account holder to view the corresponding cancelled or archived resource regardless of row status; for guests, this right belongs only to `booked_by_user_id`. Former organizers of unofficial private/community-driven resources also retain archived access. Same-community staff can read public archives; private archives additionally require private-view authority or an independent organizer/historical relationship. The final user instruction supersedes the former staff-only archive filter and cutoff. Show the Archived filter to eligible historical users/organizers as well as staff, and enforce the same record scope on direct/API/feed reads. Invitation alone grants no private archived access. Event registration does not grant every child table/chat; historical table access uses that table's relationships. See the [canonical audience rules](03-database-schema.md#cancellation-and-archive-visibility).

## Canonical staff roles and owner protections

[accepted staff-assignment column contract](16-intended-access-matrix.md#staff-assignment-columns--accepted-2026-10-30) grants full assignment-column reads to the holder and same-community owner/effective can_grant_staff managers, including audit UUIDs/timestamps. Distinguish own paused/revoked row reads from effective privileged operation checks. Historical audit access and permission mutation authority are independent; preserve owner protections and trusted role/identity/audit transitions.

Current October 30 refinement: [staff, restoration and activity decisions](16-intended-access-matrix.md#october-30-follow-up--staff-restoration-and-activity-access) supersede older D-104/105/107 and owner exceptions below. All effective staff, including owner, require approved community standing. Ownership atomically establishes the approved community account and active owner assignment; owner discipline/suspension/inactivity is prohibited. Community suspension/removal revoke non-owner staff atomically; voluntary inactivity pauses active non-owner staff. Manual restoration retains separate paused-resumption and revoked-reappointment permissions. can_discipline_users clears the community restriction; previously approved members then self-reactivate; audit access is separate.

Store management authority in `tenant_staff_assignments`, with `owner` and `admin` roles. Ordinary participants do not need a staff assignment; hosting is a relationship to a table, not a global role. Staff assignments reference global user accounts. All effective assignments, including owner, require an approved same-tenant community account but no profile. Ownership must establish that account and the active owner assignment atomically.

- The user account that creates the tenant automatically becomes its owner.
- Each tenant must have exactly one active owner. Multiple simultaneous owners are not allowed; other active staff assignments may be admins. The implemented initial flags are `can_approve_users` and `can_create_admin`; target D-106 renames the latter to `can_grant_staff` and adds `can_discipline_users`/`can_discipline_staff`. Other accepted flags include `can_manage_settings`. Owner participation deactivation is prohibited by the October 30 requirement; owners cannot have inactive or suspended community standing.
- `can_approve_users` is a non-null boolean on `tenant_staff_assignments`, default false for non-owners and always true for owners. Effective active same-tenant staff with this flag may approve pending community accounts and explicitly reinstate removed accounts under D-104. Reinstatement changes removed/inactive to approved/active on the existing row, retaining audit history; it does not resume paused/revoked staff or restore cancelled/withdrawn resources. The flag grants no suspension/removal, suspension-lifting, settings/permission editing, staff resumption, or entitlement authority.
- Initially all administrative permissions default to false for non-owner staff, including future permission flags. The admin role itself grants no administrative capability. A later community-staff configuration UI will let the owner set default permissions for new admins and customize permissions for each individual admin. Configurable defaults are deferred and apply to new admins, without silently changing existing individual grants. Neither defaults nor individual customization may disable owner permissions.
- Every owner administrative permission must initialize to and remain true, including permissions later introduced on staff assignments or related tables. Owner creation and ownership transfer set all required permissions to true atomically. Database enforcement must reject false/null owner permissions and deletion of required permission records. Adding permissions must backfill existing owners to true and extend enforcement; admins default to false. These permissions do not grant paid participation benefits or bypass platform-level restrictions.
- `can_grant_staff` (planned rename of `can_create_admin`) is a non-null boolean on `tenant_staff_assignments`, default false for admins and always true for the owner. Creating an admin assignment or issuing an admin invitation requires this permission on an active same-tenant staff assignment. New admins start with all permissions false; the creator cannot copy their own grants or grant permissions through this operation. Permission configuration remains owner-controlled. This flag also authorizes explicit resumption of a paused admin assignment with its retained permissions only when the target's same-tenant community account is approved, never automatic resumption on community reactivation. It does not authorize revoked-staff reinstatement, staff removal, permission edits, or ownership changes. Validate invitation recipient, tenant, expiry, one-use redemption, and issuing authority atomically; recipients cannot supply their own role or permissions. Invitation delivery/UI details remain open.
- Ownership is immutable after creation in the initial version, even for the owner: defer both the transfer command and UI. Owner-controlled transfer is planned for a later version; it must atomically replace the current owner, preserve exactly one active owner with all administrative permissions true, and maintain protections under concurrency. Admins cannot self-promote or transfer ownership. Revocation and global account deletion must not leave a tenant ownerless.
- An admin may never remove or block the owner from their tenant. This includes demoting, revoking, suspending, or deleting the owner's assignment or otherwise denying their tenant management access.
- An admin may also never remove or block the community account associated with the owner of that same tenant. This includes suspension, removal, deletion, or an equivalent access-denying standing change. Identify the protected account by matching both tenant and the current owner's global user ID; do not rely on profile presence or a role on the community account.

Management commands and RLS require an active staff assignment, the operation permission and an approved same-tenant community account, including owners. Apply this to every privileged read/write. Voluntary community deactivation pauses active non-owner staff; owners cannot deactivate or be disciplined/suspended. Community suspension/removal revoke non-owner staff atomically. Reject privileged access for stale/inconsistent non-approved assignments. Ownership must establish the approved community account and active owner staff row atomically.

D-106 adds `can_discipline_users` and `can_discipline_staff`, non-null booleans, false for non-owners and always true for owners. The former permits same-tenant non-owner account removal and explicit selection of that target's guest bookings for cancellation within its confirmation. It grants no unrelated booking edits or private-resource reads. The latter permits active/paused non-owner staff → revoked, with audit retention, without changing community standing, cancelling bookings, editing grants, or reappointing staff. Neither permission bypasses owner protections. Account removal requires both `can_discipline_users` and `can_discipline_staff` on the same acting staff member when the target assignment is active or paused; no assignment or already revoked requires only `can_discipline_users`. Validate live authority and target status atomically, rejecting the whole removal if either required grant is missing.

Disciplinary account removal revokes active or paused staff atomically (D-107), unlike voluntary deactivation's pause. Revoked staff reappointment is an explicit owner-only command with fresh permission review and an active same-tenant target community account, preserving identity/audit history. Neither `can_grant_staff`, paused Resume, repeated creation, nor `can_approve_users` reinstatement revives revoked grants. Plan a later refinement for specifically trusted non-owner reappointment; no such delegation exists initially. Revoking staff alone does not remove their community account or invoke removal's booking/resource consequences.

Use the [planned staff-permission migration](03-database-schema.md#planned-staff-permission-migration) to preserve legacy `can_create_admin` values in `can_grant_staff` and coordinate permission helpers, RPC parameter changes, grants, owner enforcement, clients/types, and upgrade tests. Do not alter already-applied migrations or leave independent legacy/new grant columns.

Admin access should use explicit commands and record reasons for sensitive actions. No platform-wide browser bypass. Staff who participate need the applicable community standing and resource registration; a staff role alone does not confer a seat.

## Tenant lifecycle

Tenants start `active` and may transition to `archived`, then back to `active` through controlled owner-only archive/restore commands. These are the only initial operational states; platform suspension is deferred.

An archived tenant denies ordinary community participation and ordinary tenant-scoped writes, regardless of participant standing or admin permission flags. The owner retains explicit management/restoration access. Authenticated users retain only their existing DM history, qualifying resource-specific event/table history, and independently authorized own-report reads. Do not place historical reads behind a blanket active-tenant/active-community-account predicate. Archiving preserves data, staff assignments, and exactly one active owner, while automatically cancelling all not-yet-started events/tables after clear confirmation. Already-started gatherings are excluded from cancellation, but the tenant still denies new chat/participation writes. Restoration does not reactivate accounts/staff, bypass standing, or reopen cancelled gatherings. Public lookup remains unavailable while archived.

Self-reactivation of approved/pending/suspended accounts changes activity only, so pending approval and suspension still apply. Removed accounts require manual reinstatement by effective active same-tenant staff with `can_approve_users`. D-104 explicitly extends that permission beyond pending approval; use a controlled reinstatement action, not self-service or a settings toggle. Tenant auto-approval settings govern creation, not reactivation of the retained account.

Voluntary deactivation requires a confirmation showing safe details of hosted/organized gatherings that have not started, the caller's own upcoming queued/confirmed bookings to withdraw, and guest bookings that will remain. On confirmation, cancel those hosted/organized resources and withdraw only the caller's own upcoming bookings in other gatherings. Preserve guest records, sponsorship, and group links; do not reject surviving guest requests solely because the booker voluntarily became inactive. Existing gathering cancellation rules still make all bookings unavailable when their parent is cancelled without rewriting their rows. New bookings require active approved standing. A trusted `starts_at > now` check excludes already-started gatherings, and changes to the displayed affected set must be revalidated.

Deactivation pauses an active non-owner staff assignment without erasing its permission flags. Every staff authorization and the public-creation pause exemption requires `status = 'active'` and, for non-owners, a matching `community_accounts.is_active = true`; paused flags confer no authority. Only active same-tenant staff with `can_grant_staff` may explicitly resume a paused admin whose same-tenant community account is active; community self-reactivation never does so, and revoked assignments are not revived by this command. The owner remains active when deactivating their participation account; this preserves ownership without allowing inactive ordinary participation. Independent organizer/location authority retains its existing scope and never arises merely from retained history.

D-105 is a continuing invariant rather than only a resumption check. Coordinate staff creation/resumption/role changes and community activity/removal changes so no committed state contains an active non-owner assignment with a missing/inactive community account. The requirement is activity, not an additional approved-standing condition; ordinary participation still requires approval. Reinstatement by `can_approve_users` does not replace the separate `can_grant_staff` staff-resumption action. Disciplinary removal atomically revokes active or paused non-owner assignments under D-107; existing revoked state is preserved.

D-108/D-109 authorize removal's coordinated personal-booking withdrawal and unofficial organizer cancellation for not-yet-started gatherings. Guest bookings remain unless staff explicitly selects them through the scoped `can_discipline_users` flow. Official gatherings stay intact and are omitted from its cancellation suggestions, remaining under collective `can_manage_official_activities` authority; later cancellation requires a separate authorized action. Existing cancellation cascades/history/notices apply without exposing private data or disciplinary reasons to attendees. Ordinary live participation/chat/address access ends immediately, while in-progress gatherings/reservations require separate authorized manager action. These removal rules do not broaden general event-edit powers.

Inactive users retain their DM history, qualifying cancelled/archived event/table history, and their own reports, but lose ordinary participant access to active gathering chats and exact addresses. Existing authorized self-only hide/read preferences remain available. Staff-history access still requires an effective staff assignment and matching private-view grants, unless the caller independently qualifies as a historical user/organizer. Global account deactivation, closure, deletion, and anonymization remain deferred for implementation. AM-06 (2026-10-28) nevertheless establishes that global deactivation removes all pending community-join requests tied to the account; approval staff must not receive them. This is distinct from community-only inactivity and from an Auth ban. Auth eligibility comes from trusted checks of Supabase Auth state, not an inference from application activity flags; an Auth ban leaves existing pending requests in the queue without an added target-ban check in queue reads or approval transitions (AM-06). Approval does not restore the banned user's protected access: the current caller helper checks live ban state. Supabase bans alone do not invalidate all issued JWTs; preserve the caller gate as access paths expand. A scheduled app-level ban-consequences job is a deferred possibility, with no schedule or effects selected.

Active same-tenant staff with `can_manage_settings` may edit `tenant_settings` flags. This includes `pause_community_events`, non-null/default false: true blocks non-staff creation of community-public events/tables, including attached tables. Active staff are exempt from this one gate but still need every ordinary creation permission; paused/revoked staff are not exempt. Existing resources and private creation are unaffected by this setting. The flag is not community archival and grants no archive/restore authority. Concurrency checks must prevent creation authorized on stale pause/activity/staff state.

## Enforcement and session lifecycle

Permission clarification (2026-10-28): `can_discipline_users` covers community-account suspension/removal; `can_discipline_staff` covers staff-account suspension/revocation. They replace the unmigrated removal/revocation names, preserve owner protections and existing combined removal requirements, and do not change separate restoration/permission-edit authority. Suspension mechanics not yet defined remain follow-up work; authority is now settled. Activity flags use `can_manage_community_activities` and `can_manage_official_activities`, preserving their event/table scopes.

`can_moderate_all_chats` is an independent authorization for all non-private community chats, including Discussion and event/table chats. For established public gathering-chat moderation, authorize effective same-tenant staff with this broad flag OR the relevant activity-management flag. Do not require both. Neither path moderates private chats/DMs or overrides personal hiding and retained evidence. The broad flag does not grant resource creation/edit/cancellation, private addresses, discipline or audit reads. It defaults false for non-owners, remains true for owners, and retains owner-only editing; the audit-flag delegation exception does not extend to it.

AM-05 roster/audit authorization (2026-10-28): standing and membership dates are community-public, with inactive/removed targets excluded from non-staff list views and only encountered through their authorized retained participation records. Active staff display role badges to all community users in authorized contexts; permission flags do not create badges. Paused/revoked assignments have no staff badge and follow ordinary account-list eligibility. This changes neither anonymous-web audiences nor archival limits.

Other-staff assignment/permission inspection requires owner authority or effective same-tenant `can_create_admin` (planned `can_grant_staff`). History access instead requires the corresponding new audit flag: `can_audit_community_accounts` for non-staff approval/discipline history, `can_audit_staff_accounts` for staff creation/discipline history. Owners retain both; flags default false for non-owners. No implicit audit grant follows from creation/approval/removal privileges, and no general private DM/report-evidence authority follows from an audit flag. Administrative history remains staff-only, distinct from public standing/dates and row attribution.

The user explicitly authorized owners and effective `can_create_admin`/`can_grant_staff` holders to manage both audit flags individually. Other staff-permission changes remain owner-only; possessing an audit flag alone does not permit delegation. Protect owner invariants and enforce permission checks per operation. Historical category handling when staff status changes remains to be specified before implementation. These are target requirements, not migrated policies or commands.

AM-04 profile/block contract (accepted 2026-10-28): either directed block denies global-profile data in both directions through every otherwise qualifying path, including direct URLs/IDs, shared-community `show_global_profile` and historical links. Enforce the denial before returning data; UI link hiding alone does not satisfy it. Keep global-profile fields out of permitted community/history joins or fallbacks between the pair. Community directories/profiles and shared posts/comments/author cards remain visible under ordinary audience, lifecycle and moderation/personal-hiding rules. Authorized historical community-profile links remain valid; historical global-profile links do not bypass the block. Omit the pair from each other's DM-recipient/direct-invitation selectors and independently enforce mutation denial. Existing private-host, guest, in-progress, retained-history and other participation rules remain unchanged; no new general co-presence ban or staff bypass is granted.

Accepted layer direction (2026-10-28): PostgreSQL grants define permitted columns per operation, with identity/audit columns never client-writable, potentially narrower UPDATE than INSERT, and usually all columns granted for SELECT subject to the intended field/audience contract. RLS with helper functions validates identity, membership and privileges. Data REST handles suitable operations; Edge Functions provide APIs for complex business logic it cannot handle; specialized database functions via RPC provide required transactional execution. Detailed policy/helper/endpoint design remains under review; archive RLS was selected November 3. See [backend access layers](06-backend-patterns.md#backend-access-layers--accepted-direction-2026-10-28). This direction does not assert that the current policies implement the intended matrix.

The 2026-10-24 direct-write migration keeps global profile/preferences verified-self-only. Community profile/preferences also require an active tenant and pending/approved standing, including an owner's own participation. These self-edits use ordinary RLS at statement time, with no explicit tenant lock; an in-flight save may finish alongside archival or an access change. Profile identity defaults to Auth and is not browser-writable. Preferences are saved together through `saveCommunityAccountPreferences(tenantId, preferences)`, currently containing only `show_global_profile`. Denied UPDATEs may affect zero rows; clients must request and check the affected row. Administrative read views enforce permissions internally, expose only the documented narrow fields, and never expand base profile reads. Implementation is local only; hosted rollout remains pending.

Use database grants plus per-operation RLS. Protect sensitive columns, and explicitly authorize transactional/privileged RPC. See [Supabase RLS guidance](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/database/postgres/row-level-security).

Use live community standing and resource registration records for participation decisions and live staff assignments for management decisions. Enforce owner protections against the current owner in the same transaction as the protected operation. Prevent recursive community-association policies using narrowly scoped reviewed helpers if needed. For definer functions, fix search path, qualify references, restrict execution/owner privileges, and check caller/resource/tenant explicitly.

During auth restoration, show loading rather than guessing community standing. On sign-out/account/tenant switch, cancel old requests, clear private caches, remove subscriptions, and ignore late responses. Reconcile access after expiry/revocation. A route slug, role badge, or hidden button is presentation rather than authorization.

Community participation approval uses `tenant_settings.auto_approve_users` (boolean, not null, default false): new joins start pending when false and approved when true. The creator's optional community account is approved during tenant creation regardless of this setting. Existing pending accounts require explicit approval by effective active same-tenant staff with `can_approve_users`; that flag also authorizes the distinct removed-account reinstatement action; changing the setting is not retroactive and cannot reinstate suspended/removed accounts. The target setting-edit authority is active same-tenant staff with `can_manage_settings`; the implemented milestone is owner-only. Existing-account self-reactivation preserves standing and never consults this setting to restart approval. Approval commands validate current standing and tenant permissions atomically. This does not make participation mandatory for staff or grant paid benefits. Payment must not silently override a suspended/pending account. If invitations are introduced, verify recipient, expiry, one-use redemption, tenant and issuing authority atomically.

## Required security cases

Test two tenants with visitors, approved/pending/suspended community accounts, nonattending guest sponsors, hosts, table managers, and other staff. Exercise automatic/manual modes through direct reads/writes/RPC, nested relationships, protected addresses, storage, and Realtime.

Specifically prove: pending requests/waitlists do not reveal addresses; sponsors without their own seats do not become participants; managers cannot act on other tenants/tables; rejected requests leave active queues and notify the booking account with authorized decision details; withdrawn groups never auto-confirm; mode/capacity/group-revision checks resist direct API bypass. Payments/member flags must have no current access effect.

Also prove: global signup requires no community account; tenant creation assigns the creator as owner atomically; a second active owner cannot be created; owner transfer/revocation/account deletion cannot leave a tenant ownerless; an owner without a community account can manage the tenant without receiving participant benefits; admins cannot remove or block either the owner or the owner's community account in that tenant through any write path, including during concurrent ownership transfer; protections follow the current owner and are scoped to the matching tenant.

For DMs, prove both endpoints must be active with approved or suspended standing in an active tenant; test permitted suspended-user DM participation and denial of event/table creation/signup and non-DM discussion writes. Inactive/pending/removed deny new conversations and messages; inactive/removed retain only their own historical reads. Test opt-out in existing threads, blocks in both directions, stable-account restrictions on rejoin, and owner/staff inability to bypass personal controls. Verify selected-evidence-only report review, server-computed gap metadata, no omitted-body access through IDs/joins/Realtime, private unread state, and no read access from event/table staff flags. Verify personal hide isolation in DMs/events/tables, wrong-user preference denial, moderator-hide precedence, scoped staff hide/unhide/preview, community-public audience checks, denied private-chat moderation despite combined management/private-read grants, retained private-view reads, absent shared DM moderation, and unchanged report evidence. Verify own-report reads, staff `can_discipline_users` scope/defaults, revocation, protected lifecycle writes, permanent evidence, and required closure notes with trusted actor/time. Verify that a personal block prevents DMs in every shared community, including a later shared community, while discipline in one tenant does not restrict that user's standing in another.

For customization, prove that only owners or appropriately privileged admins can edit/reset content or approved tenant details; owners need no participation account, but active non-owner staff require a same-tenant active account; private copy is not publicly readable; unsupported content keys are rejected; and clients cannot forge creation/update actors.

For identity/profile and slug rules, prove that signup needs no profile; community profiles cannot reference another tenant's account or duplicate a tenant/user pair; users cannot edit someone else's profile or gain access through profile changes; and private profiles remain protected. Prove uppercase/reserved/duplicate slugs are rejected by persistence rules, concurrent creation cannot duplicate slugs, and post-creation slug changes are denied even for owners.

For signup verification, prove that unverified identities cannot access authenticated app operations, including direct tenant creation/join/admin-invitation commands; verification followed by successful authentication returns new signups home without automatic community actions; and expired/invalid verification links allow a clear retry/resend flow. Verify the confirmation requirement in local, non-production, and production configuration.

For public tenant lookup, prove that only active tenants and the seven approved fields are returned, archived tenants are unavailable publicly while owners retain authorized access, and anonymous direct/alternate reads cannot expose excluded fields or related administrative/profile records. No public directory/search interface is shipped in this milestone.

For staff permissions, prove that a newly assigned admin starts with all administrative flags false and cannot perform administrative actions merely by having the admin role. Explicit grants authorize only their corresponding operations. New permission migrations must preserve the false default for admins and the always-true owner invariant.

For admin creation/invitation, prove that `can_grant_staff` is required on an active same-tenant assignment, new admins cannot inherit or receive caller-supplied permissions, and creation/invitation cannot assign the owner role. Validate invitation redemption without permitting recipient escalation. In the initial version, reject ownership changes through all application write paths; transfer and concurrent-transfer cases above are future acceptance requirements for the planned transfer feature. Also test voluntary deactivation pausing only active non-owner assignments, retained-but-ineffective grants, no implicit staff resumption, manual paused-admin resumption through effective active `can_grant_staff` only after target community reactivation, denial for revoked assignments, and the active-owner exception. Enforce the same prerequisite on initial non-owner assignment and ongoing authorization; test missing/inactive/cross-tenant targets, deactivation/removal races, direct-write denial, and owner exemption. Also verify independent removal/revocation checks, protected owners, active/paused → revoked on removal, revoked-only owner reappointment with fresh grants and active participation, and denial of legacy Resume/create/reinstatement bypasses. Test the removal permission matrix: active/paused staff targets require both flags; targets with no assignment or revoked status require only can_discipline_users. can_discipline_staff alone never removes a community account. Concurrent staff creation/resumption and actor-grant changes must not bypass the combined check; denial has no partial domain effects.

For account deletion, prove that referenced accounts cannot be hard-deleted through direct database writes or an Auth deletion cascade, that dependent records and human audit attribution remain intact, and that no deletion can leave a tenant ownerless. An account referenced only by audit fields must still be protected.

For tenant lifecycle, prove that new tenants start active; only the owner may archive/restore; archived tenants deny ordinary participation and writes even with admin grants; and the owner can still perform explicit management/restoration. Verify independent `can_manage_settings` authority, false non-owner/true owner defaults, account-reactivation standing preservation, removed/inactive invariants, and pause-creation denial/exemption without additional permission grants.
