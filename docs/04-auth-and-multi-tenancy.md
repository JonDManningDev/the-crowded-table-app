# Auth and multi-tenancy plan

Status: initial auth/account/tenant scope and permissions established; broader MVP privileges and lifecycle details remain proposed. The initial milestone boundaries below take precedence over broader future-feature access examples.

The 2026-10-15 accepted target retains community profiles and adds global user profiles plus profile submission during joining (D-111/D-112). D-113–D-115 settle separate community visibility/global search/global matchmaking flags and account-level blocking with private labels. Remaining audience/lifecycle/UX details are Q-016. Global-profile self-management and preference persistence are implemented and deployed to the testing database; broader audiences and join changes remain planned. See [implementation status](12-implementation-status.md).

Current domain policy (2026-10-12): payments and paid memberships are deferred to a late implementation stage. Until then, no payment, entitlement, membership-expiry, or paywall condition may govern app functionality. Community accounts and approval/standing remain required; this is not public access to private data. Historical paid-access proposals below are future-only.

## Initial milestone authorization scope

Owners can select admins from existing active (`is_active = true`) `community_accounts` in their tenant under D-105, linked to verified global users. The assignment flag (`can_create_admin` in the implemented milestone; target `can_grant_staff` under D-106) also permits active same-tenant admins to perform this controlled assignment. Enforce the target's same-tenant community association and verified identity server-side; create an admin assignment with all permissions false without changing community standing or benefits. Repeated creation must not overwrite existing assignments or their permissions. Staff identity retains its direct global-account FK; active non-owner assignments also require the conditional active-community-account invariant throughout their lifetime. Owner participation remains optional.

Support this workflow with a narrow administrative candidate list available only to the owner or staff with `can_grant_staff`: same-tenant account user ID, activity, standing, existing staff-assignment status, and optional profile display name. Omit bio, email, audit actors, and unrelated profile data; a missing profile does not prevent selection. Similarly, approval and reinstatement workflows may expose same-tenant pending/removed account IDs, activity/standing, and optional display names to effective active staff with `can_approve_users`; restrict these projections to their management purpose. These are explicit administrative projections, not general directory/profile access. Profile reads/edits otherwise remain self-only for this milestone; directory access waits for community audience/privacy policies, not paid entitlements.

The implemented owner-only backend command changes `can_approve_users` and `can_create_admin` on active admin assignments; D-106 plans the latter's rename to `can_grant_staff` and extension for the new removal/revocation grants; retain audit records and the always-true owner invariant. The full staff configuration UI and configurable default grants come later. The implemented milestone keeps tenant-detail/settings and website-copy editing owner-only. The 2026-10-14 target change delegates `tenant_settings` flag editing through `can_manage_settings`; tenant details and website-copy editing remain owner-only. `can_grant_staff` additionally authorizes manual resumption of paused admins, without granting permission-editing authority.

Implemented community-account commands cover joining and approval only; D-097–D-103 add planned deactivation/reactivation and their coordinated consequences. Suspension/removal/reinstatement commands are deferred; rejoining cannot reset standing. Admin invitations (including their persistence, delivery, redemption, and UI) are a follow-up feature. References to invitation/transfer tests elsewhere in this document describe future acceptance requirements, not additional first-milestone scope. Use `tenant_admin_actions` as the canonical tenant-scoped administrative audit table.

## Identity, standing, benefits, and participation

Supabase Auth identifies users. Community activity/standing, explicit staff grants, and resource participation are distinct. Active non-owner staff require an active same-tenant community account; owners are exempt. An active account alone grants neither staff permissions nor approval to participate. Paid membership is a deferred concept and is not part of current authorization.

Proposed tenant: an independently operated community, initially Tegucigalpa. Locations and member tables are not tenants. Community business authorization is tenant-specific, even for a user associated with multiple communities; global-profile self-management and main-account blocks are account-scoped. Future independent matchmaking/hosting require their own resource audiences and commands, not an assumed community association. The intended UX includes self-service tenant creation as described below. A tenant switcher is not currently required for the first release.

The initial sign-in method is email/password through Supabase Auth. OAuth/OIDC sign-in is planned for a later release and is outside this milestone. Supabase Auth remains authoritative for credentials and login identities; do not duplicate passwords or verification tokens in application tables. The email/password implementation must include password recovery and reliable PWA callback behavior. See [Supabase Auth](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/auth).

Initial hosted testing uses project `fsnipgsrlclbnfepcbvz` with a localhost frontend (`http://localhost:5173`). Authentication emails are English initially; multilingual templates are deferred. Resend is selected for initial SMTP delivery, and `thecrowdedtable.app` was purchased through Cloudflare Registrar. See the [Auth operations record](11-auth-operations.md) for the recommended sender subdomain, dashboard settings, and review triggers. See the [hosted setup status](../supabase/README.md#selected-hosted-testing-environment) before treating this as an operational environment.

After a new signup is successfully authenticated, send the user to **My Home**, the personal application landing page (2026-10-03 clarification), rather than a community's home page. For this initial flow, do not resume a pre-signup destination, send the user directly to community creation, or automatically create a tenant/join a community/accept a staff invitation. Account creation and community onboarding remain separate explicit actions. Password recovery retains its dedicated recovery flow rather than being treated as a new-signup redirect.

Email verification is required to complete account creation. Explicitly enable email confirmation in every environment, including local development. Signup initially shows a check-your-email screen with a resend option; only after verification and successful authentication does the user proceed to the home page. Auth and a corresponding `user_accounts` record may be provisioned before verification, but these are pending signup records, not authorization to use authenticated application features. Do not add a duplicate application-level verification flag; Supabase Auth is authoritative.

Until verified, users may browse permitted public content and complete verification/recovery flows, but may not create tenants, join communities, accept admin invitations, or perform authenticated management/participation actions. Enforce this boundary through Auth configuration and backend authorization rather than UI controls alone. Verification is a platform-wide requirement and does not replace community approval or grant membership benefits. Callback route names, environment-specific redirect allowlists, production sender/SMTP configuration, and recovery details remain implementation/setup decisions; the post-signup destination is settled as home.

## Account and community creation

1. A first-time visitor can browse permitted public content without an account.
2. **Create Account** starts email/password signup, provisioning the Auth identity and its global `user_accounts` record. Require email verification before signup is complete and authenticated app access is available; then send the authenticated user to My Home. No community account or tenant association is required.
3. **Start New Community** creates the tenant and automatically assigns the signed-in creator as its single active owner through `tenant_staff_assignments`. These writes must succeed or fail together in a trusted transaction.
4. During creation, ask: **“Would you also like to join this community as a participant?”** Supporting text: “You can manage this community either way. You can join later.” Under D-112, opting in shows editable community `display_name` and `bio`, pre-filled from the caller's global profile, and submits them with creation. Create the approved `community_accounts` row and its curated profile in the same transaction as tenant/owner/settings creation. Opting out creates neither a community account nor a community profile and leaves any global profile unaffected; the owner retains management access.
5. Joining an existing community uses the same editable pre-fill and submits the community profile with the join request. The verified caller's new account/profile commit together, with pending/approved standing determined by community policy. Missing global fields can be entered directly; joining does not implicitly save or alter a global profile. Existing-account return/reinstatement retains its separate standing-preserving policy and cannot silently overwrite an existing profile.

There is one global login. Staff assignments and community participation are separate tenant-specific relationships. Joining does not automatically grant paid membership benefits. The creator's optional community account is automatically approved during tenant creation regardless of `tenant_settings.auto_approve_users`. Create the default settings row with the tenant and owner assignment in the same transaction.

The implementation now has global and community profile tables, with real global-profile editing; community joining still permits later profile setup. D-111 adds `user_account_profiles` keyed by the global account, while retaining curated `community_account_profiles` keyed by tenant/user. Both initially have `display_name`, optional `bio`, and standard audit fields. D-112 changes new UI joins to submit account/profile together; legacy profile-less accounts require a setup path. Profile presence still grants no participation or staff authority, and account creation itself does not require a completed profile. Global-profile edits depend on verified account ownership rather than a tenant's lifecycle; community-profile writes retain community lifecycle/standing gates.

The self-controlled `community_accounts.show_global_profile` flag defaults false (D-113). It controls global-profile visibility through that community's authorized audience, independently of its curated community profile. Global `user_accounts.user_is_discoverable` defaults true and controls whether other users can find the account in standard global account searches. Separate global `user_accounts.enable_matchmaking` defaults true and controls participation in global, non-community matchmaking (D-114). All are non-null booleans; the two global flags belong on the account, not the profile or community-account row.

Do not combine the three flags into one visibility predicate. Search opt-out does not deny community-authorized global-profile reads or global matchmaking participation; community visibility does not grant searchability or matching eligibility. Matchmaking opt-out is independent of ordinary search and community matchmaking. Each feature must enforce its own live preference plus applicable audience, account, and block rules. None grants DM/invitation permission or access to unrelated affiliations/history. Remaining viewer qualification, cross-community composition, result fields, and revocation/cache behavior are Q-016; the flag names/defaults/scopes are settled. Until the feature APIs ship, preserve existing self-only account/profile reads and narrow administrative projections.

Blocks, discipline, and private label targets resolve the internal `user_accounts.user_id` even when the global profile is hidden. Names, bios, rejoining, label edits, and preference changes cannot bypass account-level blocks; staff disciplinary authority remains local. D-115 selects private labels and account-level blocking instead of a public recognition ID. Only a label's owner can read/manage it; neither the labelled user nor community staff receive access. Labels require no block and grant no additional access to target profiles, affiliations, or reports. Authorize the encountered context before displaying private recognition. D-116 settles search/matching, direct-invitation, and joining restrictions below; residual lifecycle questions remain Q-010. Any future claim of limited linkability must account for identifiers exposed by APIs, author metadata, and subscriptions, not just rendered UI.

When either account blocks the other, exclude each from the other's global account searches and global matchmaking, and deny direct invitations between them in both directions. These restrictions are pair-specific and independent of `user_is_discoverable`, `enable_matchmaking`, `show_global_profile`, or invitation opt-in. They do not make either account unavailable to unrelated users or revoke community standing. Each person manages only their own directed block; removing one does not clear a reverse block.

Personal blocks do not prevent community joining or otherwise accessible participation outside D-118's private host/participant exclusion. Private events and tables deny admission if the actual host/organizer and participant have a block in either direction, through every route including links, third-party invitations, and parent-event registration. Use resource hosting, not community ownership, generic staff authority, or audit authorship. Other ordinary attendees' blocks do not create a joining veto. D-118 extends the earlier private-table rule to private events and automatically removes existing counterpart participation/requests and rejects related invitations in not-yet-started resources when confirming a block under D-119; existing-match lifecycle remains Q-010.

D-119 evaluates the actual attendee, never the booking account as a proxy for a guest. Guests can participate independently despite a block involving their sponsor, including guest-only bookings in the other account's private gatherings. Preserve guest rows, group/booker links, and ordinary authorized guest-booking management when removing a sponsor's own participation. A block against the sponsor alone cannot reject or cancel guest participation. In-progress events/tables, existing participation/invitations, and their normal authorized access are unaffected by the block action; new-interaction gates remain separate from retained participation rights.

The block preview must tell the caller which not-yet-started private events/tables hosted by the target they will automatically leave and which not-yet-started private events/tables they host will automatically lose the target's participation. Show related mandatory invitation rejections and dependent seats. These exact automatic transitions are authorized by the block policy and cannot be deselected; they grant no general resource-management or staff power. Show only safe authorized details. Manual per-item withdrawal/cancellation/denial/invitation controls in this interface are limited to not-yet-started non-private resources and remain subject to the caller's normal permissions. Unrelated third-party private gatherings are not swept into automatic removal merely because both accounts attend, and receive no manual cancellation controls in this block interface.

Final confirmation applies the block, mandatory private effects, and optional selected non-private actions consistently; dismissing the prompt changes nothing. No manual selection is required, but all mandatory private effects still apply. Remove affected pending and confirmed participation/requests only for not-yet-started resources and reject pending invitations, preserving rows/history; withdrawing an event registration clears that attendee's linked upcoming table seats without touching guests or in-progress participation/required access. Recheck live lifecycle and start times at confirmation and refresh the preview if a gathering has started. Those removals end participation-based live resource/chat/address access while independent staff/organizer and retained historical audiences keep their own rules. Unselected non-private, independent guest, in-progress, and unaffected third-party private records remain under normal authorization. Unblocking restores none of the cancelled/rejected items; new participation/invitations require current eligibility.

Tenant slugs are lowercase, unique, exclude reserved application names, and are immutable after creation in the initial version. Validate creation at the backend/database; ownership does not permit slug changes. The migrations enforce 3–63 ASCII letters/digits with single separating hyphens and enumerate reserved route names; keep the reserved list synchronized with routing changes.

## Public tenant lookup and future discovery

Anonymous visitors may look up an active tenant by slug and receive only `id`, `slug`, `name`, `country_code`, `state_province`, `city`, and `timezone`. These are public identity/general-location fields. Exclude operational status, audit timestamps/actors, staff assignments and owner identity, permission flags, tenant settings, community accounts, and profiles. Website copy follows its own destination-page visibility. Enforce this limited representation in the backend/database rather than filtering full tenant rows in the browser; alternate reads must not bypass it.

Archived tenants are unavailable through public lookup, with a generic “Community unavailable” page rather than disclosed archive status. Authenticated owners retain management/restoration access; authenticated historical users also retain their own DMs and qualifying event/table history through Archived communities, with per-resource audience checks. Initial public access is lookup by slug; a “Find Your Next Community” page and general community listings/search are later-release features.

A future opt-in interface will let community-account holders share selected profile data on community pages and in searches. Potential controls differentiating visibility to fellow community members versus non-members remain to be designed. No sharing UI, visibility flags, or public profile-search access is included in this milestone. Profile creation is not consent to public sharing, and current private-directory restrictions remain applicable.

## Event and table access — decisions of 2026-10-12

The [event/table schema direction](03-database-schema.md#events-tables-locations-and-seats) supersedes the older activity-subtype and paid-member-only participation assumptions where they conflict. Events published to the community are visible to anyone viewing it. Cancellation/archive refinements below were confirmed on 2026-10-13. Participating account holders must be approved and not banned in that community; current `suspended`/`removed` standing fails that condition. Invite-only events, invitations, tables, private addresses, and profile data do not become public through this rule. Event approval and capacity remain required; payment and paid membership checks are deferred.

Approved account holders can create guest bookings when `tables.allow_guests` is true (default true); false blocks new guest bookings/rebookings while preserving existing ones and their ordinary eligibility. Guest names are optional. Sponsors may register guests without registering themselves for the event or table; the app does not require them to attend/play or implement community/table sponsor-attendance policies. Use one event registration per attendee and one table participation per person, tracking the booking account separately. Event-associated participation references the occupant's exact registration; standalone participation stores the attendee account or optional guest name directly. A sponsored guest is not the same concept as the older matrix's signed-in, nonpaying account, and no guest seat grants a login, community standing, or independent chat/profile access.

Only an authorized manager of the table may edit `allow_guests` or explicitly remove its guest bookings. The flag edit grants no removal authority and performs no cancellation. The UI offers optional selection of existing guests when disabling the flag, plus manual removal through standard table management at any time. Removal affects selected table participations only; it does not confer authority to cancel event registrations or bookings at other tables. Private-view permission and cancellation/archive moderation of a public user event do not grant table-editing or guest-removal powers. Official-resource management instead requires `can_manage_official_events`.

The occupant's approved event registration must precede seating at an associated table; a nonattending sponsor needs no attending registration. Explicitly cancelling an attendee's registration clears that person's linked table participations; an explicit group cancellation covers every selected registration. For unofficial resources only event organizers may add tables initially; public moderation alone is insufficient. Official event/table creation and management require staff with `can_manage_official_events`. Cancelling the event or table itself leaves registration/participation rows unchanged; current access must also account for the resource and parent lifecycle.

Groups must be accepted together in one atomic transaction. Authorize the operation against the complete persisted group, validate every booking/attending account and guest's applicable eligibility, and check full capacity before any member becomes approved/confirmed. A caller cannot bypass group acceptance by supplying a subset of registration/participation IDs. The booking-account relationship grants no staff powers and does not treat a nonattending sponsor as a seated active-table participant. Guest bookers do receive cancelled/archived history and read-only chat access under the accepted historical-access rule, but no participant-based address access. See the [accepted identity and group structure](03-database-schema.md#per-person-registration-and-participation--accepted-structure).

Seat confirmation uses `tables.manually_confirm_seats`, default false. Automatic tables confirm eligible, non-withdrawn whole groups in receipt order as capacity allows. Manual tables require an authorized manager's approval, rejection, or issue notification; the manager may select requests at their discretion but cannot bypass full capacity, event approval, or atomic grouping. Neither mode requires booker reconfirmation or uses an expiry/response window.

Rejected requests leave the active queue completely, with retained decision history. The booking account receives a prompt decision notice with server timestamp, rejecting user, and optional reason. Expose this actor identity through a narrow authorized decision projection, not unrestricted staff profile/email reads. Issue notices leave the request pending. Only table-management authority permits these actions; private-view and unofficial-resource cancellation/archive powers remain insufficient.

Cancelled community-public events/tables remain discoverable to anyone, including anonymous visitors, through a Cancelled filter and safe public details; private resources preserve their original organizer/invitee audience and authorized staff oversight. Historical registration/participation rows additionally authorize their account holder to view the corresponding cancelled or archived resource regardless of row status; for guests, this right belongs only to `booked_by_user_id`. Former organizers of unofficial private/community-driven resources also retain archived access. Same-community staff can read public archives; private archives additionally require private-view authority or an independent organizer/historical relationship. The final user instruction supersedes the former staff-only archive filter and cutoff. Show the Archived filter to eligible historical users/organizers as well as staff, and enforce the same record scope on direct/API/feed reads. Invitation alone grants no private archived access. Event registration does not grant every child table/chat; historical table access uses that table's relationships. See the [canonical audience rules](03-database-schema.md#cancellation-and-archive-visibility).

## Canonical staff roles and owner protections

Store management authority in `tenant_staff_assignments`, with `owner` and `admin` roles. Ordinary participants do not need a staff assignment; hosting is a relationship to a table, not a global role. Staff assignments reference global user accounts. Active non-owner assignments require an active same-tenant community account but no profile; owner assignments require neither a community account nor profile.

- The user account that creates the tenant automatically becomes its owner.
- Each tenant must have exactly one active owner. Multiple simultaneous owners are not allowed; other active staff assignments may be admins. The implemented initial flags are `can_approve_users` and `can_create_admin`; target D-106 renames the latter to `can_grant_staff` and adds `can_remove_users`/`can_revoke_staff`. Other accepted flags include `can_manage_settings`. Owner participation deactivation leaves the owner assignment active.
- `can_approve_users` is a non-null boolean on `tenant_staff_assignments`, default false for non-owners and always true for owners. Effective active same-tenant staff with this flag may approve pending community accounts and explicitly reinstate removed accounts under D-104. Reinstatement changes removed/inactive to approved/active on the existing row, retaining audit history; it does not resume paused/revoked staff or restore cancelled/withdrawn resources. The flag grants no suspension/removal, suspension-lifting, settings/permission editing, staff resumption, or entitlement authority.
- Initially all administrative permissions default to false for non-owner staff, including future permission flags. The admin role itself grants no administrative capability. A later community-staff configuration UI will let the owner set default permissions for new admins and customize permissions for each individual admin. Configurable defaults are deferred and apply to new admins, without silently changing existing individual grants. Neither defaults nor individual customization may disable owner permissions.
- Every owner administrative permission must initialize to and remain true, including permissions later introduced on staff assignments or related tables. Owner creation and ownership transfer set all required permissions to true atomically. Database enforcement must reject false/null owner permissions and deletion of required permission records. Adding permissions must backfill existing owners to true and extend enforcement; admins default to false. These permissions do not grant paid participation benefits or bypass platform-level restrictions.
- `can_grant_staff` (planned rename of `can_create_admin`) is a non-null boolean on `tenant_staff_assignments`, default false for admins and always true for the owner. Creating an admin assignment or issuing an admin invitation requires this permission on an active same-tenant staff assignment. New admins start with all permissions false; the creator cannot copy their own grants or grant permissions through this operation. Permission configuration remains owner-controlled. This flag also authorizes explicit resumption of a paused admin assignment with its retained permissions only when the target's same-tenant community account is active, never automatic resumption on community reactivation. It does not authorize revoked-staff reinstatement, staff removal, permission edits, or ownership changes. Validate invitation recipient, tenant, expiry, one-use redemption, and issuing authority atomically; recipients cannot supply their own role or permissions. Invitation delivery/UI details remain open.
- Ownership is immutable after creation in the initial version, even for the owner: defer both the transfer command and UI. Owner-controlled transfer is planned for a later version; it must atomically replace the current owner, preserve exactly one active owner with all administrative permissions true, and maintain protections under concurrency. Admins cannot self-promote or transfer ownership. Revocation and global account deletion must not leave a tenant ownerless.
- An admin may never remove or block the owner from their tenant. This includes demoting, revoking, suspending, or deleting the owner's assignment or otherwise denying their tenant management access.
- An admin may also never remove or block the community account associated with the owner of that same tenant. This includes suspension, removal, deletion, or an equivalent access-denying standing change. Identify the protected account by matching both tenant and the current owner's global user ID; do not rely on profile presence or a role on the community account.

Management commands and RLS require an active staff assignment, the operation's explicit permission, and, for non-owners, a matching active community account. Enforce this prerequisite for every staff read/write, including report handling, private-event oversight, settings, approval/reinstatement, staff creation/resumption, and the public-creation pause exemption. Voluntary deactivation pauses non-owner staff; the owner remains active. Missing/inactive non-owner participation must fail closed even if a stale assignment still says active. Owner protections apply on all backend write paths and remain correct during future ownership changes.

D-106 adds `can_remove_users` and `can_revoke_staff`, non-null booleans, false for non-owners and always true for owners. The former permits same-tenant non-owner account removal and explicit selection of that target's guest bookings for cancellation within its confirmation. It grants no unrelated booking edits or private-resource reads. The latter permits active/paused non-owner staff → revoked, with audit retention, without changing community standing, cancelling bookings, editing grants, or reappointing staff. Neither permission bypasses owner protections. Account removal requires both `can_remove_users` and `can_revoke_staff` on the same acting staff member when the target assignment is active or paused; no assignment or already revoked requires only `can_remove_users`. Validate live authority and target status atomically, rejecting the whole removal if either required grant is missing.

Disciplinary account removal revokes active or paused staff atomically (D-107), unlike voluntary deactivation's pause. Revoked staff reappointment is an explicit owner-only command with fresh permission review and an active same-tenant target community account, preserving identity/audit history. Neither `can_grant_staff`, paused Resume, repeated creation, nor `can_approve_users` reinstatement revives revoked grants. Plan a later refinement for specifically trusted non-owner reappointment; no such delegation exists initially. Revoking staff alone does not remove their community account or invoke removal's booking/resource consequences.

Use the [planned staff-permission migration](03-database-schema.md#planned-staff-permission-migration) to preserve legacy `can_create_admin` values in `can_grant_staff` and coordinate permission helpers, RPC parameter changes, grants, owner enforcement, clients/types, and upgrade tests. Do not alter already-applied migrations or leave independent legacy/new grant columns.

Admin access should use explicit commands and record reasons for sensitive actions. No platform-wide browser bypass. Staff who participate need the applicable community standing and resource registration; a staff role alone does not confer a seat.

## Account deletion and audit retention

Initially, referenced user accounts cannot be hard-deleted. This includes references from tenant ownership/staff assignments, community participation, and audit actor columns. Enforce restrictive deletion behavior in the database and ensure Auth identity deletion cannot cascade around it. Never null historical human attribution to enable deletion: `NULL` in `created_by`/`updated_by` means system, not a deleted or unknown user. Ownership transfer alone does not make an account deletable if other references remain. The eventual anonymization/auth-deletion workflow is deferred until explicitly designed; do not expose a deletion flow that would violate these rules.

## Tenant lifecycle

Tenants start `active` and may transition to `archived`, then back to `active` through controlled owner-only archive/restore commands. These are the only initial operational states; platform suspension is deferred.

An archived tenant denies ordinary community participation and ordinary tenant-scoped writes, regardless of participant standing or admin permission flags. The owner retains explicit management/restoration access. Authenticated users retain only their existing DM history, qualifying resource-specific event/table history, and independently authorized own-report reads. Do not place historical reads behind a blanket active-tenant/active-community-account predicate. Archiving preserves data, staff assignments, and exactly one active owner, while automatically cancelling all not-yet-started events/tables after clear confirmation. Already-started gatherings are excluded from cancellation, but the tenant still denies new chat/participation writes. Restoration does not reactivate accounts/staff, bypass standing, or reopen cancelled gatherings. Public lookup remains unavailable while archived.

## Community customization permissions

The application supplies the general website structure and behavior. Owners may edit approved community details and predefined website-copy slots; admins require explicit corresponding privileges rather than receiving all customization rights from the `admin` role alone. Delineate permissions for editing community details, editing website copy, and changing operational settings separately when the privilege model is finalized. Tenant operational status uses controlled lifecycle commands, not an ordinary unrestricted details form.

Authorize customization through active `tenant_staff_assignments` even if the editor has no community account or profile. Validate allowed content keys and text limits server-side. Read permissions match the destination page: private Community content must not be exposed through public website-content listings. Audit attribution uses trusted `created_by`/`updated_by` values referencing global user accounts, following the [schema conventions and content model](03-database-schema.md#canonical-naming-and-audit-conventions). Resetting an override to its application default is also an authorized write operation.

## Address and communication protection

Private location details are separately stored. Active participant-based access requires confirmed participation and ordinary tenant/resource authorization. Cancellation of the table or parent event immediately ends that address access without changing participation rows. Historical, archive, and read-only chat permissions never satisfy the address check. Pending requests/waitlists never qualify through participation. Preserve the authenticated organizer's existing location-specific management access where applicable after cancellation/archival; this is an in-app user, not an external venue owner or a new guest role. No general staff address exception is introduced. Neither event-management flag nor private-view permission grants addresses by itself; staff who independently qualify as an authorized organizer use that relationship. Data already seen cannot be made unknown.

No exact address in broad payloads, public pages, analytics, logs, notifications, or unrestricted Realtime events. Use authenticated resource links. Prevent general access through joins, views, functions, storage metadata, or subscriptions.

Authorize event/table chat reads and sends separately. Active event chat uses approved event registration/organizer authority; table chat uses its own participation/host authority. Event-chat access requires no table seat and does not confer access to child-table chat. Event cancellation makes the event chat and affected table chats read-only; table cancellation affects only its own chat. Historical registrants/participants and guest-registering accounts retain resource-specific reads after cancellation/archive, alongside organizers/authorized staff; any row status qualifies for that historical relationship. Archived-resource viewers retain read-only chat indefinitely. Clear send capability and participant-based exact-address state on cancellation while keeping authorized history. General platform/account/tenant restrictions remain distinct from registration status.

Personal hide/unhide is a self-only view preference in all three chat types. It neither changes source content nor anyone else's visibility; staff participants can use it only as ordinary users for their own views. Retained-history readers may manage their preferences even while sending is disabled. DM shared hiding and staff inspection overrides are prohibited; report access stays limited to submitted evidence.

Event/table shared hide/unhide and View Message require a community-public parent resource and an active same-tenant staff assignment with the matching `can_manage_community_events` or `can_manage_official_events` flag, based on the actual resource classification. Under D-110, attached tables derive that classification from their same-tenant event; standalone tables use their own stored classification. Use this rule for all resource-management and disciplinary-removal checks as well. Neither client input nor the host's staff status overrides it, and classification inheritance grants no additional private-resource reads. That flag supplies scoped moderation reads for community-public chats. Private event/table chats are categorically excluded, even for owners or staff with management and private-view permissions or their own participation/history relationship. Existing `can_see_private_events` read-only chat access remains intact; authorized private-chat viewers have only personal hide/unhide controls.

For eligible community-public chats, View Message explicitly reveals the retained body to an authorized moderator without changing any personal or shared state. Shared Unhide preserves every user's personal hide choice. Check current audience, staff flag, and tenant/resource on every action, including cancelled/archived chats. This grants no chat posting, exact-address access, anonymous chat access, or new event/table data-editing authority.

Moderator-hidden bodies must be withheld from ordinary backend/API/Realtime projections, including the author's own view; raw queries, joins, quotes, and previews cannot bypass suppression. The moderation preview uses a separate authorized path. Personal state remains private to its owner. Source IDs, chronology, and report evidence are preserved.

DMs are MVP within one community. Both endpoints reference same-tenant `community_accounts` and must be active and approved in an active tenant for creation/sending/receiving. A staff assignment without a community account is insufficient. Inactive/pending/suspended/removed accounts cannot exchange new messages; inactive/suspended/removed accounts retain read-only access to conversations they already belonged to. Use separate history-read and send predicates so a standing change cannot accidentally erase that accepted access. No historical read grant authorizes other conversations. Event/table private-view and management permissions never grant ordinary DM reads.

The accepted general participant design keeps membership separate from private per-user state. Initial one-to-one behavior comes from a UI selecting one recipient and a server creation command accepting one recipient and deriving the sender from authentication. Grants/RLS prevent bypass through direct conversation-creation or membership writes; no conversation kind, stored pair, or database count constraint/trigger enforces a two-person maximum. Group membership commands are deferred until their authorization rules ship. Recheck both endpoints, the recipient's `allow_direct_messages` (default true), and blocks on creation/every send. Opt-out prevents incoming DMs in existing threads too; bilateral blocking stops both directions but preserves history. Requests for senders with no prior DM history are a later, community-account-scoped feature.

Resolve one continuing DM conversation per unordered main-account pair and tenant through authorized create-or-get commands; current send restrictions must not block separately authorized historical reads or cause creation of a replacement thread. Concurrency control belongs to the command, preserving the general participant schema.

The accepted 5,000-character message validation and 30-message/minute budget apply across DM/event/table sends under the authenticated main account; the 10-new-DM-recipient/hour budget must also survive community/device switching. Quota accounting is server-only and cannot reveal cross-community contact history. It is not a substitute for resource-send permission.

Each participant owns only their own mute and read state. Muting is private, conversation/community-specific, and does not revoke send/receive consent or delete content; it suppresses DM alerts while preserving conversation-list unread state. Coalesced in-app notices contain no message body and grant no additional conversation access. Self-owned display/read acknowledgments must never publish read receipts, and mute state must not be readable by the peer or unrelated staff.

Block/disciplinary targets are main `user_accounts` identities. Preserve tenant standing/restrictions and audit history across rejoin attempts; tenant auto-approval must not undo discipline. Personal DM blocks apply across all shared communities; staff discipline applies only within its issuing tenant and follows the same main account on rejoin. Targeting a global identity does not grant staff global sanction powers. Personal refusal of DMs does not remove an owner's management access, and owner administrative protections do not bypass recipient controls.

Initial DM reporting exposes only selected message snapshots and server-computed gaps: each omitted sender/character length and the omitted count, never omitted bodies. Active same-tenant staff with `can_handle_reports` may view and handle reports; the submitting user may view their own report, evidence, status, and resolution notes independently of DM send eligibility. Staff need no conversation membership; non-owner staff must have active same-tenant community accounts, while owners remain exempt. The flag defaults false for non-owners and is always true for owners. Reviewers cannot use source IDs to fetch the conversation; reported users have no automatic access to reports about them.

Reports and all related data are retained permanently. Viewing never automatically acknowledges a report. Acknowledge Report manually moves submitted → under_review with trusted actor/time; Close Report manually moves under_review → resolved and requires nonblank resolution notes saved with trusted resolving actor/time. These commands require `can_handle_reports`, not report ownership alone, and cannot rewrite submitted evidence or erase records. Revocation removes staff-derived access without deleting anything; submitters retain their own read grant. The report flag grants no chat-visibility override or disciplinary powers. Shared hiding and moderator inspection apply only to community-public event/table chats under the matching management permission; private event/table chats and DMs have personal hiding only. Recommend permitting self-protection/report submission from retained read-only history. Participant-only personal state remains private.

Conversations/messages, user state, reports, notifications, and Realtime retain tenant/conversation scope. Recipient discovery uses an authorized community-account projection, not unrestricted global accounts/profiles. See the [DM schema proposal](03-database-schema.md#community-direct-messages--accepted-scope-and-proposed-structure). Ordinary DM history has no automatic expiry under D-096; usage monitoring/budget alerts do not grant new reads or revoke existing historical access. Community deactivation/reactivation and archived-community historical access are settled in D-097–D-103; D-104/D-105 settle reinstatement authority and active non-owner staff prerequisites. D-106–D-109 settle removal permissions/consequences and reviewed reappointment; their remaining implementation boundaries are in Q-010. Group membership/consent, global account deactivation/deletion/anonymization, and future read receipts/typing remain separate work.

## Community-account activation and settings — accepted target

`community_accounts.is_active` defaults true and is independent of standing. Voluntary deactivation preserves standing; disciplinary removal sets removed plus inactive. Self-reactivation of approved/pending/suspended accounts changes activity only, so pending approval and suspension still apply. Removed accounts require manual reinstatement by effective active same-tenant staff with `can_approve_users`. D-104 explicitly extends that permission beyond pending approval; use a controlled reinstatement action, not self-service or a settings toggle. Tenant auto-approval settings govern creation, not reactivation of the retained account.

Voluntary deactivation requires a confirmation showing safe details of hosted/organized gatherings that have not started, the caller's own upcoming queued/confirmed bookings to withdraw, and guest bookings that will remain. On confirmation, cancel those hosted/organized resources and withdraw only the caller's own upcoming bookings in other gatherings. Preserve guest records, sponsorship, and group links; do not reject surviving guest requests solely because the booker voluntarily became inactive. Existing gathering cancellation rules still make all bookings unavailable when their parent is cancelled without rewriting their rows. New bookings require active approved standing. A trusted `starts_at > now` check excludes already-started gatherings, and changes to the displayed affected set must be revalidated.

Deactivation pauses an active non-owner staff assignment without erasing its permission flags. Every staff authorization and the public-creation pause exemption requires `status = 'active'` and, for non-owners, a matching `community_accounts.is_active = true`; paused flags confer no authority. Only active same-tenant staff with `can_grant_staff` may explicitly resume a paused admin whose same-tenant community account is active; community self-reactivation never does so, and revoked assignments are not revived by this command. The owner remains active when deactivating their participation account; this preserves ownership without allowing inactive ordinary participation. Independent organizer/location authority retains its existing scope and never arises merely from retained history.

D-105 is a continuing invariant rather than only a resumption check. Coordinate staff creation/resumption/role changes and community activity/removal changes so no committed state contains an active non-owner assignment with a missing/inactive community account. The requirement is activity, not an additional approved-standing condition; ordinary participation still requires approval. Reinstatement by `can_approve_users` does not replace the separate `can_grant_staff` staff-resumption action. Disciplinary removal atomically revokes active or paused non-owner assignments under D-107; existing revoked state is preserved.

D-108/D-109 authorize removal's coordinated personal-booking withdrawal and unofficial organizer cancellation for not-yet-started gatherings. Guest bookings remain unless staff explicitly selects them through the scoped `can_remove_users` flow. Official gatherings stay intact and are omitted from its cancellation suggestions, remaining under collective `can_manage_official_events` authority; later cancellation requires a separate authorized action. Existing cancellation cascades/history/notices apply without exposing private data or disciplinary reasons to attendees. Ordinary live participation/chat/address access ends immediately, while in-progress gatherings/reservations require separate authorized manager action. These removal rules do not broaden general event-edit powers.

Inactive users retain their DM history, qualifying cancelled/archived event/table history, and their own reports, but lose ordinary participant access to active gathering chats and exact addresses. Existing authorized self-only hide/read preferences remain available. Staff-history access still requires an effective staff assignment and matching private-view grants, unless the caller independently qualifies as a historical user/organizer. Global account deactivation, closure, deletion, and anonymization are deferred.

Active same-tenant staff with `can_manage_settings` may edit `tenant_settings` flags. This includes `pause_community_events`, non-null/default false: true blocks non-staff creation of community-public events/tables, including attached tables. Active staff are exempt from this one gate but still need every ordinary creation permission; paused/revoked staff are not exempt. Existing resources and private creation are unaffected by this setting. The flag is not community archival and grants no archive/restore authority. Concurrency checks must prevent creation authorized on stale pause/activity/staff state.

## Enforcement and session lifecycle

Use database grants plus per-operation RLS. Protect sensitive columns, and explicitly authorize transactional/privileged RPC. See [Supabase RLS guidance](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/database/postgres/row-level-security).

Use live community standing and resource registration records for participation decisions and live staff assignments for management decisions. Enforce owner protections against the current owner in the same transaction as the protected operation. Prevent recursive community-association policies using narrowly scoped reviewed helpers if needed. For definer functions, fix search path, qualify references, restrict execution/owner privileges, and check caller/resource/tenant explicitly.

During auth restoration, show loading rather than guessing community standing. On sign-out/account/tenant switch, cancel old requests, clear private caches, remove subscriptions, and ignore late responses. Reconcile access after expiry/revocation. A route slug, role badge, or hidden button is presentation rather than authorization.

Community participation approval uses `tenant_settings.auto_approve_users` (boolean, not null, default false): new joins start pending when false and approved when true. The creator's optional community account is approved during tenant creation regardless of this setting. Existing pending accounts require explicit approval by effective active same-tenant staff with `can_approve_users`; that flag also authorizes the distinct removed-account reinstatement action; changing the setting is not retroactive and cannot reinstate suspended/removed accounts. The target setting-edit authority is active same-tenant staff with `can_manage_settings`; the implemented milestone is owner-only. Existing-account self-reactivation preserves standing and never consults this setting to restart approval. Approval commands validate current standing and tenant permissions atomically. This does not make participation mandatory for staff or grant paid benefits. Payment must not silently override a suspended/pending account. If invitations are introduced, verify recipient, expiry, one-use redemption, tenant and issuing authority atomically.
