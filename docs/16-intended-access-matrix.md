# Intended access matrix

**Removal after voluntary deactivation (G-06c, accepted 2026-11-03).** After voluntary deactivation, staff with `can_discipline_users` may record a disciplinary removal. Keep standing inactive and preserve `first_approved_at`. Set `has_disciplinary_restriction` to true and record the removal in the same transaction. Reactivation remains blocked until authorized staff clear the restriction. If the member has a paused staff assignment, also require `can_discipline_staff` and revoke the assignment atomically.

**Discipline of a pending applicant (G-06b, accepted 2026-11-03).** Reject suspension of a never-approved pending applicant. Authorized staff may remove the applicant directly. In one transaction, close the pending join request, set standing to `inactive`, set `has_disciplinary_restriction` to true and record the removal. Keep `first_approved_at` null. After clearance, the applicant may reapply under the current joining policy.

**Feature lifecycle effects (G-05c, accepted 2026-11-03).** Before a feature accepts its first records, its database package must implement and test every applicable effect of blocking, discipline, deactivation and archival in the originating transaction. A failure must roll back both the initiating action and its resource changes. Use the accepted rules for that resource, including staff-selected cancellations during suspension.

**Historical community profile access (G-05b, accepted 2026-11-03).** Each feature authorizes the message, event or other record when serving it and derives the profile link from the associated community account. The account migration provides a single-profile lookup by tenant and target. On opening the link, check current viewer eligibility, tenant and target standing, and archive rules. No source ID, receipt or repeat source authorization is required. An eligible viewer may reopen a known link even after losing access to the original record. Inactive accounts remain excluded from ordinary directory, search and unfiltered base-table reads.

**App-wide matches and competitions (G-05a, accepted 2026-11-03).** The account and security migration can proceed independently. A separate database package will deliver app-wide match and competition records together with the checks that authorize global-profile reads from those records. Verify that access against actual match and competition records before marking it complete.

**Existing audit records (G-04d, accepted 2026-11-03).** Preserve every original audit entry. Assign each history category independently when the historical evidence establishes it. An assignment insert proves creation and belongs in both histories. A generic assignment update establishes staff history only unless further historical evidence proves a dual-history transition. Entries with neither category established remain visible only to the community owner. A person’s current role is not historical evidence of a transition.

**New audit events (G-04c, accepted 2026-11-03).** Classify each new account audit event by what changed, regardless of the member’s staff role. Membership requests, approvals, standing and restrictions belong to community-account history. Staff assignments, roles and permissions belong to staff-account history. Assignment creation, active → revoked and revoked → active appear in both histories. When one transaction changes both accounts, record linked events and authorize each read independently.

**Revocation of paused staff (G-04b, accepted 2026-11-03).** A `paused → revoked` staff assignment change appears only in staff-account history. If the same transaction disciplines the community account, record that action separately in community-account history.

**Suspension history (G-04a, accepted 2026-11-03).** Community-account suspension and its reason belong only to community-account history, including when the member holds a staff assignment. In the same transaction, record an active-to-revoked assignment change as a separate linked event in both histories. A staff-history reader can see that change. Reading the suspension reason requires community-history access, including when following the event link.

**Standing reference (G-03f, accepted 2026-11-03).** One application-wide `public.community_standing` table defines standing capabilities for every community. Signed-out and signed-in clients may read all definitions. Only migrations may change them.

**Return eligibility — 2026-11-03:** Previously approved inactive accounts may self-reactivate when has_disciplinary_restriction is false, including after forced removal. If first_approved_at is null, the current joining policy applies once unrestricted. An active restriction always blocks reactivation. When staff with `can_discipline_users` clear the restriction, the same transaction closes any pending reactivation request and preserves its audit history. The account stays inactive until the member chooses to return. No additional return_requirement or repeat staff approval; earlier reinstatement descriptions are superseded on this point.

Prepared 2026-10-27 from documented product requirements. Status: requirements synthesis for discussion, not an approved replacement schema or migration. The user requires database design to serve intended product use independently of frontend completion. Current restrictions are compared with that target, not treated as its source. Recommendations in the discussion section are explicitly unaccepted.

Scope: all nine existing application tables, the three flagged views, their current operations, and the planned domains that establish their future audiences. Supabase-owned `auth.users` is included as a dependency, not an application-owned table. Planned table names below remain conceptual where the schema document calls them proposals. Commercial features, group-DM operations and other explicitly deferred features are not new implementation scope.

## Reading the matrix and sources

Accepted implementation direction (2026-10-28): operation-specific column grants (identity/audit never client-writable; UPDATE may be narrower than INSERT; SELECT usually all columns), RLS with identity/membership/privilege helpers, Data REST where sufficient, Edge Function APIs for complex business logic where needed, and specialized RPC database functions for transactional requests. The SELECT convention remains subject to the matrix's explicit field/audience boundaries. Detailed design remains under review; the archive enforcement layer was settled November 3 (RLS); no grant/endpoint migration is approved here. See [backend access layers](06-backend-patterns.md#backend-access-layers--accepted-direction-2026-10-28).

- **Settled** means an explicit accepted product requirement or its documented access contract. **Open AM-xx** identifies a decision below; it does not mean either blanket permission or permanent denial.
- **Current** describes deployed/repository behavior verified during this investigation; it is not the target requirement. **Recommendation** is a proposed answer, never an inferred approval.
- A read grant concerns both rows and fields. Reading a name does not imply reading its account settings, affiliations, audit actors, or Auth record. Reading a resource does not imply editing it. The matrix specifies outcomes without prescribing a function for every read.
- **Self** is the verified account holder. **Participant** follows the resource/action contract: new community-public participation requires approved standing, while private activities permit approved or suspended standing under the October 30 refinement. **Effective staff** means an active same-tenant assignment with the operation permission and a matching approved community account, including owners. October 30 supersedes D-105 activity-only eligibility and the optional/inactive owner-account exception. Owners cannot be disciplined or suspended/inactive; ownership establishes the approved community account and active owner assignment atomically. Owner protections remain mandatory.
- Public access includes anonymous visitors and people who have not completed verification. D-030 restricts authenticated actions, not otherwise-public browsing. Account/Auth eligibility, tenant status, community standing/activity, resource relationships and staff authority are independent checks.
- Accepted history exceptions must not inherit the active-participation predicate. Read-only history can still permit the viewer's own hide/read/mute state where explicitly allowed.

Primary sources: [decision register](08-decisions-and-open-questions.md), [vision and feature audiences](01-vision-and-concept.md#app-sections-and-desired-functionality), [authorization](04-auth-and-multi-tenancy.md), [schema](03-database-schema.md), [backend behavior](06-backend-patterns.md), and [delivery acceptance criteria](09-pwa-and-delivery.md#acceptance-matrix). ## Lifecycle and relationship rules already settled

### Report handling and direct discipline — accepted 2026-10-31

Fold the formerly proposed can_handle_reports into can_discipline_users. There is no separate report-review-only role or flag in the target schema. The earlier flag was never migrated, so omit it entirely rather than adding an alias, compatibility helper or obsolete column.

Effective same-tenant can_discipline_users now authorizes report lists/details and permitted evidence, manual acknowledgment, resolution with the required notes, and the already-accepted user-disciplinary actions and restriction clearance. Reports are the normal workflow, but authorized staff may act directly without a report. No mandatory report, acknowledgment, closure or fixed investigation sequence is a prerequisite to discipline. Direct actions retain the same actor/target checks, owner protections, structured action records and trusted audit attribution. The app supplies tools for staff judgment rather than imposing an arbitrary process.

Preserve these independent boundaries:

- Report access reveals the selected evidence and authorized gap metadata, not whole private conversations or omitted message bodies. Existing submitter reads and permanently retained evidence remain.
- Suspending/removing an active or paused staff target still requires both can_discipline_users and can_discipline_staff; independent staff disciplinary operations retain their own established authority. Neither flag bypasses owner protections.
- Browsing community-account or staff-account disciplinary history still requires the corresponding audit flag. Reading/handling a report, including its authorized resolution notes, does not grant the general audit timeline.
- Report closure and disciplinary action are distinct operations. Reports can be resolved without punishment; direct discipline does not manufacture, acknowledge or close a report automatically. Existing report-state transition rules still govern actions taken on a report.
- This flag does not add shared chat hiding, message rewriting, evidence deletion, general staff-permission editing or staff restoration. Other operation-specific permissions and lifecycle checks remain.

Linking a disciplinary action to its originating report may be supported, but it cannot be required for all actions. The physical linkage design remains implementation work. The combined permission remains false by default for non-owners and always true for owners; ordinary permission delegation remains owner-controlled. Requirements only; no migration/application deployment accompanies this decision.

### Suspension and clearance — accepted 2026-10-30

- Suspending a community account with an active or paused non-owner staff assignment requires both can_discipline_users and can_discipline_staff on the acting staff user, matching removal's combined-permission rule. Suspension and staff revocation commit together. For no assignment/already revoked, can_discipline_users suffices. Owner protections remain.
- Clearing the restriction on a suspended account returns standing to approved under can_discipline_users, without a separate can_approve_users requirement. Clearing it on an inactive account leaves it inactive; a previously approved member may then self-reactivate. Neither transition restores staff assignments or cancelled resources automatically.
- Suspension must not prevent private activities. Private activity creation/reservation and existing private commitments remain available subject to their ordinary resource, invitation, block, capacity and lifecycle rules. Do not use a blanket approved-only activity predicate to exclude suspended users from private activities.
- Block suspended users from new community-public activity creation and new community-public reservations. Existing community-public commitments are not automatically cancelled by suspension. The disciplining staff user may select each planned/registered community-public activity individually in the suspension interface. For the target's hosted activity, the applicable action is activity cancellation; for another person's activity, it is withdrawal of the target's reservation, not cancellation of everybody's activity. Preserve ordinary cancellation/cascade/history rules, independent guests and unrelated participants; no general unrelated-resource management power is granted.
- These suspension rules supersede the earlier blanket activity prohibition and do not replace the separately accepted removal/deactivation cascades. Retain independent DM, Discussion and event/table chat authorization; private-activity eligibility alone is not a new chat exception. Exact ongoing/in-progress cases and cancellation preview mechanics follow the relevant resource contracts rather than an automatic removal cascade.
- The standing lookup/helper design must express private-activity eligibility separately from new community-public participation. The proposed suspended enable_standard_participation=false cannot be used to deny every activity action. Physical capability-column/helper mapping is design work; no additional flag name is approved here.
