### Profile controls and recognition — accepted 2026-10-15

Use account-level blocks and private labels. Labels follow the target's underlying account across presentation changes/communities and remain private to their owner; editing/deleting one does not change blocking or reveal hidden affiliations. D-116 excludes the pair from each other's global searches/matches and direct invitations. D-118 automatically removes counterpart participation/requests and rejects invitations for not-yet-started private events/tables hosted by either account, and denies new admission along that relationship. D-117's confirmation lists mandatory private effects separately from optional authorized not-yet-started non-private actions, all in place. D-119 preserves guests' independent eligibility, even with a blocked sponsor, and leaves in-progress events/tables and their existing access unaffected by the block action. Unselected non-private and unaffected third-party private participation retain normal rules; remaining lifecycle/shared-space questions are Q-010.

The public identification-code proposal was not adopted. It would supply every observer a common cross-community correlation key; private labels avoid introducing that public reference. Private recognition is still not a guarantee of unlinkability. The design rationale follows the correlation concerns in the [W3C Privacy Principles](https://www.w3.org/TR/privacy-principles/); inspect existing exposed user IDs and author/audit metadata before making stronger privacy claims.

The settled controls are `community_accounts.show_global_profile = false`, `user_accounts.user_is_discoverable = true`, and `user_accounts.enable_matchmaking = true` by default. Treat them independently: visibility through a community, standard global account search, and global matchmaking are separate features. For example, false searchability plus true community visibility still permits the community-authorized profile read; false searchability plus true matchmaking still permits otherwise eligible global matching. None is a contact permission or an exception to an applicable block. Every read still needs object-level authorization, consistent with [OWASP's IDOR prevention guidance](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Insecure_Direct_Object_Reference_Prevention_Cheat_Sheet.html). Preventing abuse through a separate new main account remains a distinct trust-policy problem.

Preserve the implemented auth/account/tenant foundation. Address Q-002 setup and applicable launch-policy decisions before operating signup publicly; Q-003/Q-004 belong to deferred commercial work. D-111–D-115 are accepted follow-up profile/join, preference, and private-label changes; resolve applicable remaining Q-016 details before the dependent feature ships. These plans do not rewrite the original migration milestone. The existing community tenant boundary, initial roles, and five community destinations remain established.
